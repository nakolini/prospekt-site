What is the best focal length for portraits?

For portraits, 85 mm on full frame is the classic: flattering faces and a simple background. On APS-C, 56 mm frames about the same, and on Micro Four Thirds, 42,5 mm.

Why these focal lengths

85 mm puts you about 1,3 m (4,3 ft) from the person for head and shoulders. From there, noses and chins keep their real proportions, and the narrow view shows only a small patch of background, so it looks calm.

50 mm is the flexible choice: half-body and full-body portraits, small rooms, and people with some of their surroundings. Fill the frame with just a face, though, and you're close enough that noses start to look bigger.

135 mm gives the tightest frame and the simplest background, but a full body needs about 7,5 m (25 ft), so it's mostly an outdoor lens. 35 mm is for environmental portraits, where the place matters as much as the person. Keep faces away from the edges of the frame.

Pytania

Is 50 mm good for portraits?

Yes, for half-body and full-body portraits, and indoors where there's no room to step back. A full body fits at about 2,8 m (9,1 ft) on full frame. For tight headshots, 85 mm flatters faces more, because you stand farther away.

What focal length is best for portraits on APS-C?

About 56 mm: on a 1.5x camera it frames like 84 mm, close to the classic 85 mm. A 50 mm lens on APS-C frames like 75 mm, also a good portrait length. On Canon APS-C (1.6x), use about 56 mm.

How far do I stand for a full-body portrait with an 85 mm lens?

About 4,7 m (15 ft) on full frame and 7,1 m (23 ft) on 1.5x APS-C, with the camera held vertically and a 1.8 m person filling 90% of the frame height. For head and shoulders, about 1,3 m (4,3 ft) on full frame.
