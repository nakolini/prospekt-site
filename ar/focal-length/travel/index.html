What is the best focal length for travel?

For travel, a ⁦24-70⁩ or ⁦24-105 mm⁩ zoom on full frame covers most trips. If you carry one prime, ⁦35 mm⁩ is the most versatile: ⁦23 mm⁩ on APS-C, ⁦17 mm⁩ on Micro Four Thirds.

Why these focal lengths

Travel mixes everything: streets, rooms, food, people and the odd landscape. ⁦24 mm⁩ fits a square or a church interior, and ⁦70 mm⁩ picks out a face or a facade. A standard zoom covers both, so you change lenses less.

A single ⁦35 mm⁩ prime is lighter and usually brighter, so you can shoot indoors and at night without a flash. It's wide enough for streets and tight enough for a person, and you walk for the rest.

On APS-C the standard zoom is about ⁦16-55 mm⁩, and on Micro Four Thirds ⁦12-40⁩ or ⁦12-60 mm⁩. They frame like ⁦24-80⁩ to ⁦24-120 mm⁩ on full frame.

Is a ⁦35 mm⁩ prime enough for travel?

For many people, yes. It covers about ⁦3.1 × 2.1 m⁩ at ⁦3 m⁩ on full frame, enough for a street scene or a small room, and it's light. You'll miss the very wide and the long shots, like a whole cathedral from inside or a face across a square.

Is a ⁦24-70 mm⁩ or a ⁦24-105 mm⁩ zoom better for travel?

⁦24-105 mm⁩ reaches farther: ⁦19.5°⁩ across at the long end, against ⁦28.8°⁩ at ⁦70 mm⁩. ⁦24-105 mm⁩ ⁦f/4⁩ zooms are also usually lighter than ⁦24-70 mm⁩ ⁦f/2.8⁩ ones, which gather more light.

What focal length is best for travel on APS-C?

A standard zoom that starts at ⁦16 mm⁩ frames like ⁦24 mm⁩ at the wide end. One that starts at ⁦18 mm⁩ frames like ⁦27 mm⁩, noticeably tighter indoors. For a single prime, ⁦23 mm⁩ frames like ⁦35 mm⁩.
