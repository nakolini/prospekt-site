Guides

35 mm vs 50 mm: which should you choose?

35 mm is wider and suits streets, travel and rooms. 50 mm is tighter and suits portraits and details. On a full-frame camera, 35 mm sees 54,4° across and covers 3,1 × 2,1 m at 3 m. 50 mm sees 39,6° and covers 2,2 × 1,4 m. From the same spot, 50 mm makes everything 1,43x bigger.

How 35 mm and 50 mm frame the same scene

Which one to choose

  • Pick 35 mm if you want one lens for most things: a room, a street, and a person with their surroundings.
  • Pick 50 mm for portraits and details. For the same framing you stand farther back, so faces look more natural and the background shows less clutter.
  • At the same aperture and the same framing of a person, 50 mm blurs the background a little more than 35 mm.
  • A 50 mm f/1.8 is usually the cheapest fast lens in any system. Fast 35 mm lenses tend to cost a bit more.

Compare them over a photo

Fragen

Should I get a 35 mm or a 50 mm lens?

35 mm is wider and suits streets, travel and rooms. 50 mm is tighter and suits portraits and details. On full frame, 35 mm covers 3,1 × 2,1 m at 3 m and 50 mm covers 2,2 × 1,4 m. Compare both over a photo in the free lens comparison tool.

How far do I stand for a full-body portrait with 35 mm or 50 mm?

On full frame, with the camera held vertically, about 1,9 m (6,4 ft) with 35 mm and 2,8 m (9,1 ft) with 50 mm. That fits a 1.8 m person in 90% of the frame height. On 1.5x APS-C, about 2,9 m (9,6 ft) and 4,2 m (14 ft).

What do 35 mm and 50 mm frame like on APS-C?

On a 1.5x APS-C camera, 35 mm frames like 52 mm on full frame and 50 mm like 75 mm. On Canon APS-C (1.6x), like 56 mm and 80 mm. For the full-frame look on 1.5x APS-C, use about 23 mm and 33 mm.
