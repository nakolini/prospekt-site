50 mm vs 85 mm: which should you choose?

50 mm is the more flexible lens: half-body and full-body portraits, small rooms and everyday photos. 85 mm is the classic portrait lens: head and shoulders, flattering faces and a simple background. On a full-frame camera, 50 mm sees 39.6° across and covers 2.2 × 1.4 m at 3 m. 85 mm sees 23.9° and covers 1.3 × 0.8 m. From the same spot, 85 mm makes everything 1.7x bigger.

How 50 mm and 85 mm frame the same scene

Which one to choose

  • Pick 85 mm if you mostly shoot portraits and have room to step back. A full-body portrait needs about 4.7 m (15 ft) between you and the person.
  • Pick 50 mm if you shoot indoors or want one lens for people and everything else. A full body fits at about 2.8 m (9.1 ft).
  • 85 mm keeps you farther from the face, so noses and chins keep their proportions. Its narrower view shows a smaller patch of background, which looks calmer.
  • Both come as affordable f/1.8 lenses in most systems. The 85 mm ones are bigger and usually cost more.

Questions

Should I get a 50 mm or a 85 mm lens?

50 mm is the more flexible lens: half-body and full-body portraits, small rooms and everyday photos. 85 mm is the classic portrait lens: head and shoulders, flattering faces and a simple background. On full frame, 50 mm covers 2.2 × 1.4 m at 3 m and 85 mm covers 1.3 × 0.8 m. Compare both over a photo in the free lens comparison tool.

How far do I stand for a full-body portrait with 50 mm or 85 mm?

On full frame, with the camera held vertically, about 2.8 m (9.1 ft) with 50 mm and 4.7 m (15 ft) with 85 mm. That fits a 1.8 m person in 90% of the frame height. On 1.5x APS-C, about 4.2 m (14 ft) and 7.1 m (23 ft).

What do 50 mm and 85 mm frame like on APS-C?

On a 1.5x APS-C camera, 50 mm frames like 75 mm on full frame and 85 mm like 128 mm. On Canon APS-C (1.6x), like 80 mm and 136 mm. For the full-frame look on 1.5x APS-C, use about 33 mm and 56 mm.
