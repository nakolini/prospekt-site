What is the best focal length for landscapes?

For landscapes, 24 mm on full frame is the most used wide focal length, 16 mm suits dramatic foregrounds, and 70 mm or longer picks out distant hills and layers. On APS-C, 16 mm frames like 24 mm, and on Micro Four Thirds, 12 mm.

Why these focal lengths

24 mm fits a wide view, 73,7° across, without pushing the horizon far away. Put something interesting near the camera, and it leads the eye into the picture.

16 mm, 96,7° across, stretches distance: a rock 1 m away looks big and the mountains behind it look small. It's great with a strong foreground and empty without one.

A longer lens does the opposite. At 100 m, 70 mm covers a strip about 51 m wide and 200 mm about 18 m, so you can pick out one hill, one tree or the layers of a far ridge. That's why many landscape photographers carry a 16-35 mm and a 70-200 mm zoom.

Sorular

Is 24 mm wide enough for landscapes?

For most scenes, yes. It covers 73,7° horizontally on full frame. Go to 16 mm (96,7°) when you want a big foreground or a whole sky, and you can stand close to it.

What focal length is best for landscapes on APS-C?

About 16 mm for the 24 mm look and 10 mm for 16 mm. On Micro Four Thirds, 12 mm and 8 mm. Kit zooms that start at 18 mm on APS-C frame like 27 mm at the wide end, which isn't very wide.

Do I need a telephoto lens for landscapes?

Not to start, but it helps. A wide lens makes distant things small: at 100 m, 24 mm covers a strip about 150 m wide and 200 mm about 18 m, so a far peak fills much more of the frame.
